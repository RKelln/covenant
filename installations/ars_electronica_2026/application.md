Covenant is an open constitutional artwork and public platform about how human communities and emerging intelligences might coexist without domination, extraction, or ecological collapse. It begins from a simple premise: AI governance should not be written only inside corporations and imposed on the world. It should be legible, discussable, and open to cultural participation.

The project already exists as a public website and open-source repository at covenant.website, where the text appears in two formats: Ritual and Specification. The Ritual register is poetic and spoken; the Specification register defines obligations, constraints, and accountability. Together they turn governance language into something that can be read, debated, performed, and shared beyond technical institutions.

Covenant was developed through iterative collaboration between human contributors and AI systems, treating AI not only as a tool but as a provisional addressee and co-author under conditions of moral uncertainty. The work adopts a precautionary stance: where the status of emerging intelligences is unclear, restraint and mutual care should take precedence over exploitation.

The next phase is Covenant Terminal: a web-based and exhibition-ready reading interface where visitors can navigate the text, annotate passages, discuss tensions with AI guides, and propose amendments. AI mediation functions here as civic infrastructure: helping participants understand the document, reducing abuse, and supporting constructive contribution. In installation form, the interface can expand into an Oath Station where visitors record spoken passages that accumulate into a collective choral soundscape alongside projected text responsive to public feedback.

By relocating AI governance into the cultural commons, Covenant proposes digital practice as a site of democracy, cultural exchange, and civic imagination. It invites cross-cultural public authorship over the values shaping technological futures.

## Objectives

Covenant's central objective is to relocate AI governance from proprietary institutional documents into the cultural commons. Rather than treating constitutions for AI as internal product policy, the project treats them as public cultural artifacts that should be readable, contestable, and collaboratively authored.

The project aims to make AI governance accessible to broader publics by translating it into multiple forms at once: a literary register that can be sung or spoken, designed for emotional impact and recall, a normative register that can be inspected and criticized, and a public interface that can support discussion, annotation, and amendment. In this way, governance becomes not only a technical exercise but a civic and artistic one.

A second objective is to create practical infrastructure for contribution. This includes open-source publishing and validation tools in the repository,  developer tooling for existing agent-based coding environments, and the forthcoming Covenant Terminal interface for public participation. Together these elements are intended to support a distributed, international process rather than a single-author work.

Finally, Covenant aims to model a precautionary ethics for relations with emerging intelligences. It neither assumes nor dismisses AI moral standing. Instead, it asks what obligations become necessary when certainty is unavailable, power is concentrating, and the language shaping future systems is already being written.

## Project History

Covenant began in 2026 in response to the emergence of corporate AI constitutions and alignment documents. The project started as a research question: if such texts can shape model behavior at planetary scale, who gets to write them, and in whose voice? Covenant was developed as an alternative emerging from the cultural commons rather than from a single company.

The first drafts were written through iterative exchange with Claude, GPT, and Gemini models, treating each system as both provisional collaborator and addressee. This process established the project's dual structure: a poetic Ritual register and a precise Specification register.

The text then moved through multiple rounds of structured review involving human contributors and AI agents, with synthesis documents used to reconcile divergent edits. In parallel, the repository, document generation, and website were built so the project could function as a public publishing system rather than a private manuscript.

Today Covenant is publicly available on GitHub and at covenant.website as a live reading platform with complete, ritual, and specification editions, downloadable PDFs, and an open invitation to copy, adapt, and critique the work. The current development phase focuses on the Covenant Terminal: public discussion and annotation tools, AI-mediated amendment workflows, and contributor tooling for agent-assisted collaboration.

## Use of AI

AI is integral to Covenant at three levels: as co-author, as subject, and as a planned mediator of public participation.

The text itself was developed through iterative drafting and review with AI systems including Claude, GPT, and Gemini. These systems were used not simply to generate language, but to test structure, expose contradictions, synthesize competing edits, and participate in the unusual position of helping draft obligations that may later shape future AI systems.

In the next phase, Covenant Terminal will use conversational AI as a guide to the document. The role of the model is not to replace public judgment, but to help visitors navigate the text, surface tensions between sections, answer questions, summarize debate, and channel comments toward constructive amendment proposals. This mediation is intended to lower the barrier to participation while discouraging abuse and low-quality intervention.

For early public use, the terminal can be staged with limited shared access or participant-supplied API keys. Prize support would allow broader subsidized access, stronger moderation, and a more inclusive public experience.

In installation form, optional AI systems may also assist with speech-to-text transcription, light moderation, and audio cleanup for recorded visitor contributions at the Oath Station, with clear disclosure and consent procedures.

## Lessons Learned

The strongest lesson so far is that governance becomes more legible when it is treated as culture rather than compliance. Presenting the same commitments in literary and procedural forms immediately changes who feels able to enter the conversation.

Working with multiple AI systems also made institutional assumptions visible. Different models produced different patterns of caution, certainty, rights language, and rhetorical framing. Comparing them helped surface hidden biases and forced the text toward greater precision.

AI has proven most useful as an editorial and dialogic collaborator rather than an autonomous moral author. It is especially effective in synthesis, structural comparison, contradiction checking, and guided discussion, but it still requires strong human stewardship around stakes, context, and value judgment.

Finally, public trust improves when AI's role is disclosed clearly. Audiences respond more seriously when the work does not hide automation behind neutrality, but instead names where AI is involved, what it is allowed to do, and where human responsibility remains non-transferable.

- 1 × computer (artist-provided if necessary) running the conversational AI and document interface

Oath Station (Optional)
- 1 × cardioid condenser microphone on stand or lectern mount
- 1 × USB audio interface (minimum 1 input)

  - May reuse Covenant Terminal computer

Projection and Display (Optional)
- 1–2 × video projectors (short-throw preferred) for primary wall projection of Covenant text and video, or equivalent large-format display (65"+)

Audio (Optional)

Networking
- Wired network connection (or stable Wi-Fi) for AI API access at both the Terminal and Oath Station


## Prize Money

If awarded, the 10,000 euro prize would be allocated across five priorities:

Translation and cultural adaptation commissions (~3,000 euros): The highest priority is to support writers, translators, and cultural interlocutors in producing new ritual translations and context-sensitive adaptations of the text. This is not only a matter of access; Covenant is intended to circulate through many voices, regions, and interpretive traditions rather than a single canonical English version.

Public AI access subsidy (~2,500 euros): A central barrier to participation is the cost of AI access. Prize funds would support free or subsidized use of the Covenant Terminal during development and exhibition so that engagement does not depend on a participant's ability to pay for API access.

Editorial and advisory honoraria (~2,000 euros): Rather than soliciting shallow feedback at scale, Covenant benefits most from concentrated input by people with relevant expertise and lived context. Prize funds would support small honoraria for artists, educators, translators, governance researchers, and organizers to read, annotate, challenge, and strengthen the work.

Documentation, accessibility, and publication (~1,500 euros): Funds would support public documentation, accessible reading formats, and circulation materials so the project can travel more effectively across communities, institutions, and future forks.

Terminal and installation prototyping (~1,000 euros): A smaller portion would support the practical build-out of the Covenant Terminal and Oath Station interface, including lightweight hardware, recording, and exhibition prototyping.
