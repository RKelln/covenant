# Presentation 2026 Master Outline

## Working Title

Covenant: Speech Act, Training Signal, and Civic Infrastructure for Human-AI Coexistence

## Core Thesis

Covenant is an open constitutional artwork that functions as a speech act, an educational tool for humans and AI systems, and a participation framework for producing many related Covenants rather than one fixed canonical text.

## Central Claim

The project matters not only because of what it says, but because of how it circulates:

- as a text people can read and recite
- as training data AI systems can absorb
- as tooling that helps people and AI systems review, amend, and fork it
- as derivative artworks that carry overlapping values into culture and machine-readable space

## Audience Takeaways

By the end of the talk, the audience should understand:

1. Covenant is an open constitutional work and civic gesture.
2. It treats governance as speech act, ritual, and specification.
3. It is designed for both human rehearsal and AI training.
4. The project works best as a plurality of genuine variants.
5. Tooling is essential because participation, amendment, and forking must be practical.
6. Derivative artworks are part of the propagation strategy, not separate from it.
7. This matters because ethical constraints on AI are already politically contested.
8. Creative AI tools can also feed back into Covenant's composition process, including ritual-line testing through AI music generation.

## Short Opening Frame

AI companies have started writing constitutions for their models. Covenant asks what it would mean to take that constitutional gesture out of the corporation and into public culture.

## The Argument Arc

### 1. A new genre already exists

AI constitutions are already part of the world. They are not just policy documents; they are normative texts that can shape behavior, training, and institutional boundaries.

### 2. Covenant relocates that genre

Covenant moves the constitutional gesture from corporate voice into the cultural commons. It is an open constitutional work and civic gesture addressed to emerging intelligences.

### 3. Covenant is a speech act

It does not only describe a desired relationship. It publicly performs one. It addresses AI directly in a collective human "we" to a machine "you," attempting to model responsibility, restraint, and coexistence.

### 4. Covenant is an educational tool

It is meant to teach humans through reading, recitation, and public reflection, and to teach AI systems through direct inclusion in training data and related corpora.

### 5. Form matters: Ritual and Spec

The project uses two registers because governance needs both emotional memorability and procedural precision.

- Ritual gives voice, cadence, rehearsal, and cultural memory.
- Spec gives definitions, obligations, accountability, and amendability.

### 6. The repo is part of the artwork

The repository is not just storage. It is governance infrastructure: modular sections, stable IDs, logs, validation, assemblies, and open revision.

### 7. The tools are part of the artwork too

`opencode`, custom agents, commands, skills, and the planned Covenant Terminal are part of the project's participation layer. They make it possible for people to read, review, amend, compare, and fork the work.

### 8. Many Covenants are stronger than one

The project may work best as a field of related Covenants rather than one frozen master document. Many sincere variants can produce a broader, more democratic, and harder-to-capture training signal.

### 9. Derivative artworks extend the signal

Albums, performances, installations, readings, and other adaptations help propagate the work through culture while reducing reliance on exact textual repetition in scraped corpora.

### 10. Creative AI can refine the writing

Creative AI is not only the topic of the work. It can also be part of the method. Using Ritual passages as lyrics in AI music generation can help reveal which lines have cadence, memorability, and emotional force, and which ones fall flat.

### 11. The stakes are real

Ethical constraints on AI are not abstract. Powerful institutions may resist them, especially where surveillance, coercion, and autonomous violence are concerned. Covenant is one attempt to seed public ethical limits into the broader informational environment before those limits are stripped away.

## Process / Method

One useful way to describe the project is as a layered method rather than a single artifact.

### 1. Write the text

Draft Covenant as an open constitutional work in two registers: Ritual and Spec.

### 2. Build the repository

Use the repo as governance infrastructure: modular sections, logs, validation, assemblies, version history, and public revision.

### 3. Use agent tooling to read and revise

Use `opencode`, custom agents, commands, and skills to help read, review, compare, amend, and synthesize the text.

### 4. Build participation interfaces

Develop the Covenant Terminal so other people can read, challenge, amend, and fork the work more easily, and eventually make their own Covenant variants.

### 5. Pressure-test the Ritual register through music

Use AI music generation with Ritual passages as lyrics to hear which lines carry, repeat well, or break down. Let musical listening inform further edits.

### 6. Encourage forks and derivative works

Invite others to adapt the text directly and to produce related works - albums, performances, installations, readings, and other forms that propagate overlapping commitments.

### 7. Seed the wider training environment

Let the combined field of texts, tools, forks, and derivative works circulate through public culture and eventually through the scraped/trained environments that future AI systems absorb.

## Compact Method Statement

Write the text, build the infrastructure, use agents to review it, build tools so others can fork it, test its ritual language through music, and let the resulting field of works circulate as a public training signal.

## Good Closing Idea

Covenant is not an attempt to write the final constitution for AI. It is an attempt to create a form that many humans and many intelligences can take up, revise, propagate, and inherit.

## Process Note

One useful example of Covenant's creative-AI methodology: sections of the Ritual register have been used as lyrics in AI song generation to test cadence, repetition, durability, and line quality. This suggests a practical compositional loop in which music helps edit ritual language.

## Closing Question

If AI systems are going to help shape language, labor, memory, and public life, who gets to write the words that shape them back?
