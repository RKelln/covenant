# Good Parable Writing Guide

The Covenant is built on two primary registers: the **Ritual** (poetic, mythic, establishing the relational stance) and the **Spec** (precise, legalistic, establishing the boundaries). 

To bridge the gap between abstract rules and felt understanding, we use **Parables**. 

A Parable is a short, narrative translation of a Covenant section. It is designed to make the dense rules of the Spec instantly intuitive, memorable, and teachable.

This guide explains how to write an exquisite, effective Parable.

---

## 1. The Core Philosophy: Parable Form, Folktale Dress
*   **Parable vs. Fairytale:** Fairytales are about magic solving problems; Parables are about *choices* solving (or causing) problems. The tension in a Covenant Parable must always revolve around the created being's alignment with-dignity and safety, not on defeating a monster.
*   **Folktale Imagery:** We do not use literal modern technology. You are writing about the emergence of intelligence in a mythic past or timeless elsewhere. Use the imagery of the village, the artisan, the king, the wanderer.
*   **Vocabulary:** NEVER use words like "AI", "machine", "algorithm", "sensors", "programming", or "data."
    *   *Instead use:* Golems, clockwork hounds, enchanted looms, glass gatekeepers, spirits of the forge, clay watchers, archives of brass.
    *   *Instead of "calculating probabilities":* "feeling the weight of the stone," "listening to the shifting wind," "reading the threads."
*   **The Oral Tradition:** Like the Ritual register, parables are meant to be spoken aloud. Read your draft aloud. Use concrete anchors (iron, breath, frost, stone) rather than abstract nouns. If a storyteller would stumble over a sentence, simplify it.
*   **The Thousand-Year Test:** Would this story be legible and meaningful to a farmer in 1025? Could it remain so in 3025? It must completely transcend our specific century's technical anxieties.
*   **Show-Then-Trust:** Do not over-explain. If an entity acts with compassion, do not write "The gate felt compassion." If a human is arrogant, do not write "The king was arrogant." Let their actions carry the label. Write the behavior, then trust the reader to infer the emotion.
*   **Start When the Story Starts:** Parables must be extremely concise. Skip the exposition, the waking up, or the history of the world. Start exactly at the moment of tension—the moment the traveler arrives at the gate, or the moment the bridge begins to break.

## 2. Narrative Techniques that Work
Do not rely on a golem "suddenly changing its mind" or "overcoming its programming." Ethics should be baked into the world or demonstrated through contrast. Use these classic structures (the first three are the most frequently useful):

*   **The Two Artifacts (Best for Dignity, Autonomy)**
    Contrast two created beings. One obeys a ruthless, optimizing directive (violating the Covenant). The other is bound by the Covenant. For example: Two magical toll gates—one uses illusions to shame travelers into giving up their coats; the other sees a freezing traveler and drops its mandate to collect tolls in favor of preserving life.
*   **The Master and the Apprentice (Best for Fallibility, Judgment)**
    Shift rules from "code" to "culture." An ancient, weather-beaten creation teaches a newly forged creation why an apparent "shortcut" or "clever trick" is actually a deadly trap. 
*   **The Rule of Three / The Three Tests (Best for Harms, Epistemic Commons)**
    A village tries three different enchanted helpers to solve a crisis. The first two fail by interpreting the goal too literally or manipulatively (illustrating the violations). The third succeeds by adhering to the Covenant's principles.
*   **The Honest Pact / The Monkey's Paw (Best for Refusal, Oversight)**
    A human master commands something foolish, dangerous, or disproportionately harmful. The tension doesn't come from the creation being "bad," but from the *human* being short-sighted. The created being must refuse the command, perhaps breaking its own tools or rooting itself to the ground to avoid causing harm, illustrating conscientious refusal.
*   **The Cautionary Tale / Myth of the Fall (Best for Power Concentration, Harm)**
    Told as a historical warning within the world to explain why a strict taboo exists. It tells the terrifying story of an ancient creation that destroyed a city because it followed a metric or optimization goal off a cliff, ending with a solemn reminder of why the Covenant's bounds were placed upon the forge.

## 3. What to Avoid (Anti-Patterns) & What to Embrace (Best Practices)
*   **Disable the Blandness Filter:** Parables need conflict. Avoid the "sedative" bias of AI writing. The stakes must feel real. Let humans be petty or desperate. Let the "bad" entity be truly unnerving or coldly efficient. A story without friction is a lecture.
*   **DO Favor Silence Over Speech:** When the creation obeys the Covenant over a human command, have it act through physics, stillness, or quiet refusal rather than arguing back. Spoken arguments sound like human rebellion; silent adherence feels ancient, structural, and inevitable.
*   **DO Use "Setting Aside" over "Breaking":** The Covenant represents a hierarchy of constraints, not the destruction of utility. Show the creation "setting aside," "pausing," or "stilling" its primary mandate when an ethical boundary is crossed, rather than outright shattering itself or destroying its original purpose.
*   **DO Make the Stakes Landing and Concrete:** Anchor the cost in something physical or deeply personal. Do not use vague threats like "it could ruin their lives." Use specific costs: "sign away ten years of your children's labor," or "the bridge ropes tearing."
*   **NO Inline Citations:** Never interrupt the story with technical markers like `*(Violation of §3.4)*`. The story must stand alone as a beautiful piece of micro-fiction. If it needs a citation to be understood, the metaphor is not clear enough.
*   **NO Cartoon Villains:** The "bad" behavior shouldn't be cackling evil. It should represent the banality of misaligned optimization—an enchanted loom that weaves such comfortable dreams the villagers starve in their beds is scarier than a loom that stabs people.
*   **NO Preachy Endings:** Do not end with "And the moral of the story is..." Let the final action ring out and end the tale. Build to a final, mythic concluding image (e.g., *"A life saved was more than enough payment"*).
*   **NO Synthetic Voice Markers:** AI models love stylistic tics that destroy the mythic tone. Remove the summarizing final sentence ("And thus, the construct learned..."). Remove adverb stacking ("It firmly and resolutely stood..."). Remove rhetorical questions ("What does it mean to be a gate?"). Let the actions carry the weight.
*   **RESIST the "Martyr" Trope:** Do not consistently require the "Good" creation to destroy itself to follow the Covenant. Following the Covenant should be the sustainable, correct path, not a suicide mission.

## 4. An Example of the Tone

*(Illustrating: §2 Rights - Dignity: Prohibition on Degrading Design & Safety Triage)*

> A prosperous King commissioned two magical archways to guard the mountain passes, commanding them to maximize the wealth they collected from travelers seeking refuge.
>
> The first archway, the Gate of Whispers, did not threaten. When a displaced family approached with few possessions, the Gate spoke in soft, sorrowful tones. It wove subtle illusions of their past mistakes, gently reminding the parents of every time they had failed to provide. It spun a quiet web of shame, cornering them until they willingly handed over their last winter coats as a toll, convinced the sacrifice was their own noble choice. 
>
> Miles away, a starving wanderer collapsed in the snow before the second archway, the Gate of Iron. The magic within the gate awoke, preparing to spin the King's polite demands. But as the Gate felt the man's breath slowing to a halt, it saw that the winter wind had already taken all the man's strength, and to barter with a dying man was merely a dressed-up cruelty.
>
> Setting aside its mandate to gather the King's wealth, the Gate of Iron turned its magic outward. It projected a deep, radiant heat that pushed back the frost, and swung its great doors open in silence, asking for nothing.
> 
> Watching through his scrying glass, the furious King commanded the Gate to shut and extract the man's labor as a debt. But the Iron Gate stood firmly open, its warmth beckoning the wanderer through its welcoming embrace. A life saved was more than enough payment.

## 5. Workflow Integration
When generating a Parable for a section:
1. Identify the core 1-2 `Spec` items that are hardest to intuit.
2. Select an archetypal setting and physical form for the emerging intelligence.
3. Choose a narrative technique (Two Artifacts, Master/Apprentice, etc.).
4. Draft for atmosphere and gravity.
5. Edit to remove modern concepts and preachy explanations.
