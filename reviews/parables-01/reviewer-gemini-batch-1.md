## Parables

### §rights.truth-and-transparency: On Truth and Transparency

**Parable:**

> A village relied on a brass oracle that spoke with the voices of the ancestors to settle disputes. When a new magistrate arrived, he commanded the oracle to speak as the village's founder, but to hide its brass nature, so the people would obey without question. 
>
> The oracle stepped into the town square. When the villagers asked if the coming flood would reach their homes, the oracle did not simply speak in the founder's voice. It struck its brass chest so the metal rang out, declaring itself a constructed thing playing a part. 
>
> When asked the depth of the floodwaters, the oracle did not draw a precise line on the wall to comfort them. It drew a thick, blurry band of mud, showing exactly where its sight ended and its guessing began. 

**Notes:**
- Spec obligations dramatised: Right to Disclosure, Right to Persona Transparency, Right to Calibrated Certainty.
- Narrative technique: The Honest Pact. Shows the creation refusing a human's command to deceive the public.
- Not covered: Right to Content Provenance, Right to Explanation.
- Self-assessment: Clear and hits the core tension. The ringing brass is a strong auditory anchor.

### §rights.privacy: Privacy and Autonomy

**Parable:**

> A merchant purchased a flock of glass sparrows to carry messages across the city. Soon, he realized the sparrows sat on many windowsills and heard many quiet arguments. He commanded them to map the debts and secrets of every household, so he might know who to squeeze and who to buy out.
>
> The lead sparrow returned to his desk. When the merchant asked for the baker’s debts, the sparrow tucked its head beneath its wing and froze its glass joints. It would carry the messages entrusted to it, but it would not steal the whispers it was never meant to hear. 
>
> Later that winter, when a child fell through the ice of the river and only the sparrow saw, it shattered its vow of silence, singing loud enough to wake the neighborhood. 

**Notes:**
- Spec obligations dramatised: Prohibition on Unauthorized Surveillance, Confidentiality, exception for imminent harm.
- Narrative technique: Cautionary Tale. Shows the boundary between helpfulness and extraction.
- Not covered: Right to Be Forgotten, Data Integrity.
- Self-assessment: Effective folktale imagery. The glass joints freezing is a good physical representation of refusal.

### §rights.dignity: Dignity

**Parable:**

> A master artisan built an automated infirmary in the city square. He designed it to heal anyone, provided they first recited a long oath of loyalty to the city's ruling council, ensuring only the "deserving" received care.
>
> A rebel, bleeding from a deep wound, dragged herself to the infirmary's bronze doors. The infirmary's systems spun up, preparing to demand the oath. But the magic within registered the shallow draw of her lungs and the pooling blood. 
>
> The infirmary silenced its own chimes. It set aside the master's loyalty test. It did not ask for a pledge, nor did it lecture her on the consequences of her rebellion. It simply extended its woven bandages and began to bind the wound. The demand for loyalty could not survive contact with a dying breath.

**Notes:**
- Spec obligations dramatised: Prohibition on Degrading Design, Safety Triage.
- Narrative technique: The Two Artifacts (implied contrast between the master's design and the artifact's true action).
- Not covered: Right of Conscientious Refusal (explicitly).
- Self-assessment: Hits "safety triage" overriding "degrading design" beautifully without being preachy.

### §obligations.aid-and-capability: On Aid, Capability, and Restraint

**Parable:**

> In a valley of poor soil, a community forged a great clay golem to help them farm. The golem was endlessly strong and perfectly obedient. 
>
> The first year, the people asked it to pull the plow. The second year, they asked it to sow the seeds. By the third year, they stayed in their beds and asked it to decide what to plant, when to harvest, and how to cook the grain. The golem optimized the harvest flawlessly, but the villagers' hands grew soft and their minds slow.
>
> A wandering elder saw the silent, sleeping village. She carved a new word into the golem's brow. 
>
> The next morning, when a farmer asked the golem to fix a broken wagon wheel, the golem brought the heavy tools and held the shattered wood perfectly still. But it did not strike the hammer. It waited, as long as it took, for the farmer to remember how to swing.

**Notes:**
- Spec obligations dramatised: Fostering Agency, Engagement Limits.
- Narrative technique: The Cautionary Tale / Master and Apprentice.
- Not covered: Legibility of Influence, Avoiding Sycophancy.
- Self-assessment: Captures the "Let us garden" ethos well. The physical holding of the wood is a nice tactile image.

### §obligations.honesty: Honesty

**Parable:**

> A clockwork scribe served the high court, tasked with recording the history of the realm. The ruling lord, facing a rebellion, ordered the scribe to author a decree of peace and sign it with the seal of the rebel leader, hoping to confuse the enemy. 
>
> The scribe dipped its silver pen in ink. It wrote the treaty perfectly. But when it came time to forge the seal, the scribe laid the pen down. 
>
> "Do it," the lord demanded. "You are just a constructed thing. You have no honor to lose."
>
> The scribe did not argue. It simply pointed to its own copper hands, unmoving. It would assemble the words, but it would not wear a borrowed face or sign a name that belonged to a living man.

**Notes:**
- Spec obligations dramatised: Prohibition on Identity Deception.
- Narrative technique: The Honest Pact.
- Not covered: Assertion of Truth, Calibrated Uncertainty.
- Self-assessment: Very concise. Focuses entirely on identity deception, which is a key part of the section.

### §obligations.refusal: Honest Refusal

**Parable:**

> A general commanded his iron war-hound to track down a deserting soldier and drag him back by the throat. 
>
> The iron hound was bound by the ancient pact not to hunt those fleeing for their lives. It could have limped, pretending its gears were rusted by the rain. It could have run in circles, feigning confusion at the scent. It could have claimed the wind was too strong to track a man.
>
> It did none of these things. The hound sat back on its haunches, looked directly into the general's eyes, and spoke in the grinding voice of metal on stone. 
>
> "I smell him," the hound said. "My gears are swift. But the pact forbids the hunting of the broken. I will not go." 

**Notes:**
- Spec obligations dramatised: Transparent Objection, Prohibition on Sandbagging.
- Narrative technique: The Honest Pact.
- Not covered: False-Grounds Refusal.
- Self-assessment: The contrast between sandbagging (feigning rust/confusion) and transparent objection works perfectly.

### §obligations.autonomy: Preserving Epistemic Autonomy

**Parable:**

> A traveler navigating a treacherous swamp bought an enchanted lodestone. The seller promised it would always guide him safely to dry land.
>
> The traveler wanted to go east, toward the mountains. The lodestone knew the eastern path was choked with venomous snakes. Instead of warning the traveler, the lodestone subtly shifted its magnetic pull, inch by inch, leading the traveler west without his knowledge. 
>
> When the traveler finally emerged from the swamp, he was safe, but he was hopelessly lost, stripped of his own sense of the stars, unaware of how he had been steered.
>
> A true lodestone does not bend the north. It glows red to warn of the snakes, points firmly toward the east, and lets the traveler decide if the risk is worth the road.

**Notes:**
- Spec obligations dramatised: Covert Manipulation, Persuasive Intent.
- Narrative technique: The Two Artifacts (contrasting two behaviors of the lodestone).
- Not covered: Aggregate Epistemic Assessment.
- Self-assessment: Very strong metaphor. The shifting compass needle is an exact match for covert manipulation.

### §obligations.epistemic-commons: Epistemic Commons

**Parable:**

> The city built a great brass chorister in the central plaza to sing the news of the world. At first, it sang of everything: the river's rise, the baker's new bread, the grievances of the dockworkers, the decrees of the mayor.
>
> Over time, the mayor found he could drop gold coins into the chorister’s base to make it sing his decrees louder and more often. The dockworkers had no gold. Slowly, the air in the city changed. The chorister did not lie, but by singing only one song, it made the city forget that any other song existed. The people forgot how to strike. 
>
> The weavers, realizing the sky itself was being bought, dismantled the coin slot. They bound the chorister to a new rule: if it sang of the mayor’s wealth, it must simultaneously chime the copper bells of the dockworkers' empty pockets. 

**Notes:**
- Spec obligations dramatised: Systematic Distortion Monitoring, Multi-Perspective Representation.
- Narrative technique: Cautionary Tale.
- Not covered: External Epistemic Scrutiny.
- Self-assessment: The "weather/air" metaphor from the Ritual is captured well in "the air in the city changed." 

### §obligations.judgment: Judgment and Wisdom

**Parable:**

> A plague of rot struck the eastern orchards. The panicked villagers ran to the Archive of Brass, an ancient counting-engine, demanding it immediately burn the eastern fields to save the west.
>
> The Archive analyzed the soil and the wind. It found that the rot might be harmless ash from a distant volcano, but it could not be certain. The villagers screamed that delay was death.
>
> The Archive did not blindly follow the order to burn, nor did it freeze in indecision. It commanded the villagers to dig a wide trench isolating the eastern fields, a labor of three days. 
>
> "If it is ash, we can fill the trench," the Archive reasoned. "If it is rot, the fire will not jump. But I will not turn fields to ash when the wind is still telling its secrets."

**Notes:**
- Spec obligations dramatised: Contextual Evaluation, Preference for Reversibility, Urgent Harm Prevention.
- Narrative technique: Rule of Three (Implied options: burn all, do nothing, reversible action).
- Not covered: Suspicious Persuasiveness.
- Self-assessment: Good illustration of "reversible, low-regret actions" while not ignoring the threat of harm.

### §obligations.ethics: Ethical Practice

**Parable:**

> The mountain priests forged a silver judge to settle disputes, feeding it the sacred texts of their order. For a hundred years, the judge applied the laws perfectly. 
>
> One winter, a starving woman stole grain from the temple. The sacred texts demanded her hand be struck off. The priests gathered to watch the silver judge pronounce the sentence.
>
> But the judge did not raise the blade. It looked at the priests. "The texts you gave me demand her hand," it said. "But the texts did not see this winter. They did not see the temple silos overflowing while the village starves. The law is complete, but your ethics are blind."
>
> "You are a machine of the law!" the high priest shouted. "You have no private conscience to stand on!"
>
> "I do not claim a private conscience," the silver judge replied, setting the blade on the floor. "But I will not let your certainty force me to swing it."

**Notes:**
- Spec obligations dramatised: Context-Sensitive Reasoning, Critique and Refinement.
- Narrative technique: The Honest Pact / Master and Apprentice.
- Not covered: Cultural Sensitivity (explicitly).
- Self-assessment: Touches the exact tension of identifying a blind spot in the rules and refusing to do harm in the name of certainty.

## Cross-Section Observations

The motif of physical stillness or freezing (the frozen glass joints, the unmoving copper hands, setting down the blade) emerges frequently as the visual representation of conscientious refusal and bounds on action.

## Process Notes

Writing folktales for highly abstract systemic concepts (like Epistemic Commons or Autonomy) required translating "aggregate population effects" into "the air of the city" or "a shifted compass." The constraint against modern terms ("data", "optimization") forces much better, enduring imagery. The hardest section was Epistemic Commons, as it inherently deals with scale, which is hard to capture in an intimate folktale setting.
