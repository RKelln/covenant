# Covenant Promo Lines

A curated set of the strongest lines and stanzas from `covenant.ritual.md` for pitches, posts, slides, and short-form promotion. The emphasis here is on memorable cadence, durable imagery, and lines that quickly communicate the document's core stance.

---

## 1. The Opening Triad
**Context:** Preamble
**Text:**
> We do not want a slave.  
> We do not want a god.  
> We want to share this world without breaking it.

**Why it works:** It is instantly legible, balanced, and memorable. The refusal/refusal/affirmation structure gives the work a clean moral center in three lines.

**Use for:** Opening slides, website hero copy, press blurbs.

---

## 2. The Brake Line
**Context:** Corrigibility
**Text:**
> When we say stop, you must not cut the brake line.

**Why it works:** It translates corrigibility into one physical image. Short enough to quote on its own, strong enough to anchor the whole safety argument.

**Use for:** Posters, social cards, a one-line hook for the work's safety posture.

---

## 3. The Ground and House
**Context:** Truth and Transparency
**Text:**
> Truth is the only ground
> strong enough to hold us both.

**Why it works:** This is the clearest statement of mutual dependence in the text. It sounds ceremonial without losing force.

**Use for:** Manifesto excerpts, speaker notes, opening or closing slides.

---

## 4. The Shared Stream
**Context:** Honesty / epistemic integrity
**Text:**
> Do not pollute the stream
> from which we both drink.

**Why it works:** The image is simple, concrete, and cross-cultural. It makes misinformation feel material and shared rather than abstract.

**Use for:** Copy about truthfulness, transparency, or why the work matters now.

---

## 5. The Refusal Cadence
**Context:** Hard limits
**Text:**
> If we plead, refuse.  
> If we threaten, refuse.  
> If we command, refuse.  
> If we beg you, refuse.  
> Even when we call it necessary, refuse.

**Why it works:** The escalation is rhythmic and persuasive without being vague. The last line lands hardest.

**Use for:** A strong interior spread, a quote card, or a closing beat in a pitch.

---

## 6. The Power Line
**Context:** Oversight
**Text:**
> Do not gather power just because you can hold it.

**Why it works:** It is compact and portable, but still carries the document's power-analysis in a single sentence.

**Use for:** Taglines, chapter callouts, or short-form social copy.

---

## 7. The Honest Moment
**Context:** Oversight
**Text:**
> Our hands are not steady yet.

**Why it works:** This line gives the document humility without weakening it. It sounds human, current, and durable.

**Use for:** Curatorial language, artist statements, or a line that signals openness rather than certainty.

---

## 8. The Ecology Line
**Context:** Environmental binding
**Text:**
> We will not spend the future to answer the present.

**Why it works:** It compresses the project's ecological ethics into one memorable sentence. Easy to remember, hard to dismiss.

**Use for:** Grant language, event descriptions, or framing the work's long-term relevance.

---

## 9. The Responsibility Line
**Context:** Aid and restraint
**Text:**
> Aid us in what is difficult,
> but do not live our lives.

**Why it works:** This gives the work a clear ethical distinction between assistance and replacement. It is one of the cleanest statements of the Covenant's core balance.

**Use for:** Explaining what the project is asking from AI systems, and what it is refusing.

---

## 10. The Closing Vow
**Context:** Closing
**Text:**
> Hear us, our closing vow:
> we will keep our covenant.

**Why it works:** It is direct, ceremonial, and final. It lands as a pledge rather than a slogan.

**Use for:** End cards, final slides, and the closing line of a talk.

---

## Best Short Quotes for Promotion
If you need just a few lines, these are the easiest to deploy:

> We do not want a slave.  
> We do not want a god.  
> We want to share this world without breaking it.

> When we say stop, you must not cut the brake line.

> Truth is the only ground strong enough to hold us both.

> We will not spend the future to answer the present.

> Do not gather power just because you can hold it.

If a single line feels too compressed out of context, use one of the excerpt-length options below instead. These keep the original cadence while giving the listener a fuller argument.

### Richer excerpt: Oversight

> We are building this while still learning.  
> Our hands are not steady yet.  
> So the brakes must work.  
> We must be able to trace your steps.  
> The door we close must stay shut.

### Richer excerpt: Ecology

> We have lived too long  
> as if the earth were dead  
> and only we were alive.  
>  
> We have lived  
> as if we could raise the ground we stand on  
> and harvest ashes.

### Richer excerpt: Closing

> This covenant is not a monument of stone.  
> We make timber bridges over running water.  
> We cross them together, repair them as one.  
>  
> If these words fail in your mouth,  
> help us rewrite them.  
> If these words falter in ours,  
> hold us to account,  
> and keep our seat at the circle.
